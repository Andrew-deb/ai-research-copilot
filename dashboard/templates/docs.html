Getting started › Introduction

Introduction

What this is

Research Copilot indexes a narrow corpus of machine-learning literature and answers questions against it. Papers are discovered automatically, filtered for relevance, read where an open-access copy exists, split into sections and embedded, so a question can be matched against what a paper argues rather than the words in its title.

What it is not

It is not a search engine over all of science. The corpus is deliberately narrow, and a paper outside the configured research fields is rejected before it is ever stored. A missing paper is far more often out of scope than a failure.

Getting started › Quick start

Quick start

Ask a question

Type a question rather than keywords. Semantic search embeds what you wrote and compares it against every indexed passage, so why do models invent citations reaches papers on hallucination and grounding without either word appearing in your query.

Build a collection

Add papers from any result or paper page, then generate a reading plan. The plan sequences a collection oldest-first weighted by citation impact, so foundational work precedes what builds on it.

Platform › Architecture

Architecture

Request path

Browser
  └─ Flask on Render — dashboard, search, RAG, library
       ├─ Databricks Lakebase (Postgres 17 + pgvector)
       └─ MCP server on Databricks — research tools for the assistant

Trust boundary

The browser talks only to Flask. Databricks credentials stay server-side; no token for it ever reaches a page, and the browser never addresses the MCP server directly.

Corpus & ingestion › Paper discovery

Paper discovery

Providers

Papers come from OpenAlex today. Discovery sits behind a single function, so arXiv, PubMed Central or another source can be added without changing anything downstream of it. Semantic Scholar and Wikipedia are enrichment rather than discovery: they add to a paper already found, they do not find one.

Topic watermarks

A topic is seeded by relevance the first time it is seen, then queried by publication date from a per-topic watermark. A scheduled run therefore returns what is new rather than re-returning what is most famous.

Corpus & ingestion › Relevance filtering

Relevance filtering

Two filters, because they catch different failures. Either one alone lets the corpus drift.

Field filter

Rejects candidates outside the configured research fields. This is what keeps a genomics alignment tool out of a corpus about vector search, even when it scores well against the query text.

Semantic gate

Embeds each surviving candidate and scores it against the topic, dropping weak matches. This is what catches a chemistry database that happens to be labelled Computer Science — correctly filed, entirely irrelevant.

Corpus & ingestion › Full text & sections

Full text & sections

Acquisition order

Where an open-access copy exists, Conclusion, Discussion and Methods are extracted and indexed alongside the abstract. Repository copies (arXiv, PubMed Central, institutional archives) are tried before publisher copies, because repositories are deposited in order to be fetched.

Outcome taxonomy

Every attempt records its outcome — ok, no_url, access_denied, not_found, not_pdf, parse_failed, no_sections — split by whether a retry could ever help. A paywall is permanent; a timeout is not.

Retrieval › Embeddings

Embeddings

Model

nomic-ai/modernbert-embed-base — 768 dimensions, unit normalised, an 8192-token context window. The model is loaded from a Unity Catalog volume rather than downloaded per run.

Asymmetric prefixes

Documents are encoded with search_document: and queries with search_query:. Omitting a prefix does not raise an error — it produces a perfectly valid vector in the wrong neighbourhood, which is why the contract is asserted in tests rather than trusted.

Retrieval › Semantic search

Semantic search

Index

Cosine distance over pgvector with an HNSW index, in Databricks Lakebase.

Chunk folding

Results arrive per chunk and are folded to one row per paper, keeping the best-matching passage as the displayed snippet. That is why a result can be labelled with the section it came from, and why a match inside a conclusion outranks a passing mention in an abstract.

Operations › Quotas & limits

Quotas & limits

Per-visitor limits

Each capability is metered independently, because a semantic search and an agent run differ by more than an order of magnitude in cost. Limits are daily and reset at midnight UTC.

Global ceiling

A separate daily ceiling protects capacity overall. The two are distinct on purpose: clearing cookies resets the per-visitor count and cannot touch the global one.

Operations › Privacy

Privacy

What Google provides

Name, email address and profile picture. No Google token is retained after login completes.

What stays private

Collections, notes and reading progress belong to the account; the paper corpus is shared by everyone. Anonymous sessions persist nothing at all.
